Research

Scores get reported, compared and acted on long before anyone checks what they identify. My work takes an evaluation apart into the score, the span it reads, the proxy label that stands in for the construct, and the rule that turns all of it into a verdict — then tests each link instead of assuming it. Predictions are fixed in dated notes before each run, and the code, per-episode records and those notes are released with every paper.

Validity
When does agreement mean understanding? A score and a cheap proxy read from the same text can agree on shared surface evidence rather than on the construct. Re-reading the proxy away from the scored span separates the two.
Scoring rules
The rule is part of the measurement. Re-scoring identical agent trajectories under rules that differ in one condition moves pass rates by tens of points, and changes how much credit an intervention receives.
Chance levels
What a pooled number can identify. In stratified evaluations a pooled AUC has no single chance level; the admissible interval can span most of the scale, with the labels alone enough to compute it.
Acting on it
Measurement that changes behaviour. Inference-time controls — routing, runtime messages, verification gates — that use a diagnostic to decide what a system does next, with no additional training.

Next I want to take the last one further in two directions. Deeper: what an intervention that reads a diagnostic can be made to guarantee, rather than merely improve on average — which requires knowing when the diagnostic itself is entitled to fire. Wider: multi-agent and reasoning systems, and settings where a wrong claim carries a real cost, so the measurement has to support a decision rather than a ranking.
